Content Refresh Prioritization

Prioritizing Webpages for Refresh Review Based on Historical Search Performance & Content Signals

Author: ML Research Engineer Intern
Dataset: FlyRank Anonymized Release (30,000 Rows)
Validation: Client-Holdout Split
Executive Abstract

Which webpages should be prioritized for content refresh based on historical search performance and content signals? Content teams have many webpages competing for limited review time. This project explores how historical search-performance signals and content signals can help prioritize pages that may warrant content-refresh review. Using an anonymized dataset of 30,000 content items across 32 pseudonymized clients, we evaluated pre-decision search metrics and structural content signals to identify pages experiencing traffic and impression decline. A transparent heuristic rule baseline achieved a Precision@50 of 0.240 on a client-holdout validation split, whereas a Random Forest classifier achieved a Precision@50 of 0.680 (0.740 in single-split evaluation) and an ROC AUC of 0.747. In time-aware and client-grouped validation splits, the model consistently demonstrated superior ranking efficiency, identifying declining high-opportunity pages with over 2.8× precision compared to hand-written rules. These observed results provide a directional decision-support tool for editorial teams to prioritize content refresh reviews, though human verification remains necessary as historical performance signals do not guarantee future traffic or ranking improvements.

2. Introduction & Problem Statement

In modern search engine optimization (SEO) and content engineering, existing organic content decays over time due to shifting search intent, algorithm updates, and competitive pressure. Content teams have many webpages competing for limited review time. This project explores how historical search-performance signals and content signals can help prioritize pages that may warrant content-refresh review.

Content teams face a continuous capital allocation decision: Should engineering and editorial resources be spent creating new articles or refreshing existing decaying content?

Manually auditing thousands of published URLs is inefficient and prone to subjective bias. Simple heuristic rules (e.g. "refresh everything older than 6 months") frequently flag high-ranking stable articles while missing visible pages suffering rapid impression drop-off.

This research addresses the question: "Which webpages should be prioritized for content refresh based on historical search performance and content signals?" We build, validate, and audit a machine learning decision-support pipeline that scores and ranks existing pages into a prioritized reviewer queue.

💡 Core Engineering Objective
Provide a decision-support ranking tool that elevates declining, high-demand content to human reviewers, maximizing editorial ROI while strictly avoiding automated publishing actions.

3. Data & Feature Preprocessing

Our empirical analysis utilizes the bundled anonymized FlyRank ML Internship dataset (data/raw/content_refresh_anonymized.csv). The dataset consists of 30,000 content items aggregated over a trailing 90-day window across 32 pseudonymized client domains.

Data Sanitization & Public-Safety Controls

To maintain strict privacy and public-safety compliance, the dataset contains NO client names, private URLs, raw query strings, IP addresses, or credentials. Identifiers are pseudonymized (e.g., client_f369cb89fc, content_304f48230142).

Feature Vector Construction (52 Features)

From the 44 raw columns, the feature engineering pipeline constructs 52 input features across three primary categories:

  • Search Performance Signals (GSC): Log-transformed 90-day impressions (log_impressions_90d), clicks (log_clicks_90d), click-through rate (ctr), average ranking position (avg_position), and impression active days (days_with_impressions).
  • User Engagement Signals (GA4): Log-transformed total sessions (log_sessions_90d), engagement rate (engagement_rate), scroll rate (scroll_rate), and AI-referred session percentage (ai_traffic_pct).
  • Content & Structural Metadata: Article word count (word_count), character count (char_count), content age in days (content_age_days), days since last update (days_since_last_update), position tiers, and content age tiers.

Target Definition & Base Rate

The target label is_declining_label is defined as 1 when the 30-day impression trend drops by more than 20% compared to the prior 30-day period (trend_direction == "down"), and 0 otherwise.

  • Declining Target Positive Rows: 16,262

4. Methodology

To establish a robust ML pipeline, we compared a hand-written heuristic rule baseline against three supervised classification models under strict validation constraints.

Week-4 Baseline Heuristic Rule

The hand-written baseline calculates a heuristic score by identifying stale content (days_since_last_update ≥ 180) with high search visibility (impressions_90d ≥ 500).

Week-5 Machine Learning Suite

We evaluated three candidate architectures using scikit-learn:

  1. Logistic Regression: L2-regularized pipeline with StandardScaler and balanced class weights.
  2. Decision Tree Classifier: Constrained tree (max_depth=5, min_samples_leaf=50) to evaluate interpretability.
  3. Random Forest Classifier: Ensemble model (n_estimators=200, max_depth=10, min_samples_leaf=25) to capture complex feature interactions.

Honest Split Design (Client Holdout)

Standard random row splits suffer from data leakage when multiple pages belong to the same website domain. To evaluate true out-of-sample generalization, we implemented client_holdout splitting: 20% of pseudonymized client domains (2,325 rows) were held out entirely for test evaluation, while 80% (27,675 rows) were used for training.

🔒 Data Leakage Audit
Target-derived trend metrics (trend_pct and trend_direction) were strictly excluded from model feature matrices. An automated leakage audit verified zero forward-window signal contamination in input features.

6. Limitations & Honest Framing

Rigorous research requires explicit boundary conditions on what the data can and cannot claim:

  • Observational Nature: The dataset captures historical performance; it does not represent controlled A/B experiments.
  • Seasonality & External Shifts: 90-day windows may conflate seasonal search demand drops with intrinsic content decay.
  • Missing Intent Context: Broad search engine algorithm changes may alter rankings regardless of content quality.
  • Generalization Boundaries: Model parameters reflect the 32 client domains studied; new domains may exhibit distinct distribution shifts.
⚠️ Mandatory Research Claim Boundary
This analysis provides directional decision-support and does not establish that refreshing a page will cause improved traffic, rankings, or conversions.

7. Ranked Action Playbook

The pipeline outputs a ranked priority queue (outputs/refresh_queue.csv) blending model decline probabilities with explicit reason codes.

Action Category Mapping

  • CTR_OPPORTUNITY → refresh_and_review_ctr: Visible pages (Positions 1–20) with CTR < 0.5% experiencing decline.
  • HIGH_DEMAND → refresh_and_review_engagement: High impression pages with low engagement/scroll rates.
  • RANKING_OPPORTUNITY → refresh: Stale pages with strong baseline demand facing active decline.
  • LOWER_PRIORITY → monitor: Stable or low-demand content requiring ongoing monitoring.
🚫 Explicit Automated System No-Go List

The output queue is strictly designed for human reviewer decision-support. Automated software systems MUST NOT automatically:

  • Publish content
  • Rewrite content
  • Delete pages
  • Redirect URLs
  • Modify canonical URLs
  • Modify robots.txt
  • Guarantee ranking improvements
  • Guarantee traffic improvements
  • Make irreversible decisions
